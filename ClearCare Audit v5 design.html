Reading Your Findings

What your scan results
actually mean for
your audit exposure.

Not all violations carry the same risk. The scan tells you what's wrong. This tells you what to do about it — and how urgently.

High Severity — Immediate Risk

These violations are the primary basis for CMS corrective action requests.

High-severity findings typically involve missing or null values for required rate fields, invalid billing code type formats, and absent required header fields. They are enumerated directly in 45 CFR § 180.50 and § 180.40.

If your file has high-severity findings, you have material exposure today. CMS does not require a prior warning before initiating a corrective action. Address these first.

Medium Severity — Partial Compliance

Your file satisfies some requirements but fails on specifics that auditors check.

Medium findings often involve field format errors (strings where numerics are required), missing optional-but-expected fields, or schema version mismatches. The file is partially compliant — but partial compliance is not a defense in a corrective action.

Resolve medium findings in the same remediation cycle as high findings. CMS reviews the file as a whole, not field by field in isolation.

Patterns Across Low Findings — Systemic Issues

Clusters of low-severity findings often indicate a pipeline or templating problem upstream.

If the same field type fails across dozens of billing codes, the issue is not in the data — it's in how the file is generated. Low findings concentrated in one field are more serious than scattered low findings across many fields.

The scan identifies patterns, not just counts. Review the distribution of low findings before dismissing them as low priority.

Audit Evidence

The record holds up
because nothing in it
can be changed after the fact.

Most compliance tools produce reports. ClearCare Audit produces records. The difference matters when CMS asks not just what your posture is, but how you know — and who decided. Hospitals that received corrective action requests in 2023 and could not produce field-level documentation faced extended remediation timelines — some spanning multiple audit cycles.

Three structural properties make the evidence defensible. They are not policy commitments. They are enforced by how the system is built.

Nothing is overwritten

Every state is preserved exactly as it existed

When a correction is applied, the original scan record stays intact. The system creates a new record alongside it. Your legal counsel can point to the exact state of the file at any point in the process — before the correction, after the correction, and at export.

Full traceability

Every action links back to a specific person and time

Upload, scan, review, authorization, and export each create a timestamped entry in the governance log. The log is permanent and append-only — new entries are added, but nothing is retroactively altered.

Reproducible findings

The same file, evaluated against the same rules, produces the same result

Scan results are pinned to a specific version of the CMS rule registry. Any party can verify that a scan result was correctly produced from a specific input and rule version — the output is not an interpretation.
